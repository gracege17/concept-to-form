# Worked Example — LSC 2027 Event (illustrative)

This is a **teaching example based on client briefing discussed in a design project**, not a public claim that a visual direction was approved. Keep factual source notes separate from the designer's proposals. Do not include contact details or internal documents in public examples.

## 01 Understand

**Client-confirmed briefing, paraphrased:**
- Event: Legal Services for Children annual fundraising event, 2027.
- Goals: fundraise, engage donors and volunteers, celebrate resilient clients and dedicated staff.
- Audience: law firm/pro bono partners, donors and community supporters.
- Desired tone: welcoming, hopeful, community-connected.
- Avoid: formal gala look, corporate feel, childish imagery, visual busyness, obvious AI styling, dark/nighttime look.
- Prefer: open layouts, natural or hand-crafted materiality; prior cut-paper city scene was liked.
- Young people: real photography excluded; teenage representation permissible; prior silhouettes were disliked.
- The event date and final copy must be confirmed before production.

**Communication problem (designer synthesis):**
Create a mature, human and welcoming invitation that makes supporters feel part of a real community while honoring young people's agency.

## 02 Observations & tensions

**Observation candidates (interpretations, not client quotations):**
1. Support involves multiple people with different roles.
2. Young people are not passive recipients of help.
3. A fundraising invitation should honor both supporters and the young people at the center.

**Tension A — Support × Autonomy:** How can a graphic show *support without control*?

**Tension B — Individual × Collective:** How can distinct people/formal elements create a shared space?

**Tension C — Quiet voice × Visible presence:** How can something small affect a larger arrangement?

Choose based on evidence and clarity — not which metaphor is easiest to image-generate.

## 03 Visual principles

For Support × Autonomy, explore without literal hands:
- **Making room:** two forms shift apart to open a clear place for a third.
- **Shared support:** two independent forms stabilize a third without enclosing it.
- **Redistribution:** one small element changes the balance of the composition.

Draw 9 black-and-white thumbnails, e.g. 3 variants for each mechanism. Keep title, frame size and hierarchy constant so mechanisms can be compared fairly.

## 04 System

After selecting one mechanism, define:
- **Fixed:** asymmetrical relationship, clear title hierarchy, generous warm-paper negative space.
- **Variable:** size/position of supporting forms within a consistent proportional range.
- **Material options (subject to brand and print requirements):** cut-paper or subtle risograph grain.
- **Exclusions:** gratuitous sun, leaves, fake scenic landmarks or extra motifs not performing a job.

Test on a 5×7 portrait invitation and a horizontal digital banner. Ensure required copy fits before finalizing.

## 05 Critique example

If an added sun appears only to make the image “hopeful,” ask whether that meaning is already conveyed by composition and color. If it adds no essential purpose, remove it. If a supporting shape resembles a ribbon but is supposed to signify lifting, inspect whether it visibly *supports* anything. Replace a vague explanation with a stronger physical relationship.

**Lesson:** A good rationale is not a license to add visual clutter. The visual must work independently of the explanation.
