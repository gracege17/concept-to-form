# Visual Operations — From thought to form

Use these as **verbs** describing what shapes do, rather than as visual styles.

| Operation | Visual question | Possible meaning | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale | What becomes unexpectedly large/small? | Amplification, importance | Spectacle without meaning |
| Connect | Where do independent forms meet? | Partnership, access | Generic connection icon |
| Overlap | What is created by shared space? | Cooperation, plurality | Visual clutter |
| Subtract | What space appears when something is removed? | Access, making room | Looks like an accident |
| Repeat | What rhythm or accumulation changes meaning? | Collective momentum | Busy decoration |
| Reframe | What changes when we shift the boundary? | A new viewpoint | Arbitrary cropping |
| Balance | How do different weights coexist? | Equity, mutual reliance | Looks static/forced |
| Transform | What becomes something else? | Change made tangible | Gimmick or unreadable object |

## Three-pass exercise

1. **Write a relationship:** e.g. “Two independent forms create a place for a third.”
2. **Draw it without named objects:** Use black/white circles, bars, negative space. Make 3 variants.
3. **Find culturally and contextually grounded material:** typography, local architecture, real objects, motions or imagery that naturally share that relationship.

One successful visual principle beats a collage of five partly explained metaphors.

## Reality check

- Can viewers recognize the subject or read the text immediately?
- Is the meaning understandable without a paragraph of explanation?
- Is a literal symbol actually necessary?
- What could this visual unintentionally imply about power, age, vulnerability or identity?
