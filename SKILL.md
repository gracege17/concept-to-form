# Concept to Form

**From meaning to form. From form to system.**

This skill helps make a **specific piece of graphic design** more thoughtful, distinctive, and visually compelling. It is not a general-purpose brand strategist. This is an independent approach informed by concept-driven design practice, not an official Pentagram skill.

## Non-negotiable: protect the task scope

**The assignment is the unit of work.** If the user asks for one invitation, poster, editorial illustration, or campaign asset, design **that asset**. Never silently expand it into a mission audit, an organizational identity overhaul, a branding workshop, or a complete brand system.

| Stage | Wrong interpretation / AI drift | What we actually need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **01 UNDERSTAND** | Unearth the organization's ultimate mission, identity, or “brand truth” | Understand the communication problem **in this design assignment** |
| **02 CREATIVE CONCEPT** | Discover the organization's timeless core value | Propose **one meaningful creative idea for this piece** |
| **03 VISUAL PRINCIPLE** | Find abstract brand symbols and metaphors | Explore **how typography, shape, scale, and space express that idea** |
| **04 GRAPHIC SYSTEM** | Invent a complete corporate visual identity | Set **the visual rules needed for the current piece and its actual deliverables** |
| **05 DESIGN CRITIQUE** | Audit alignment with a company's long-term brand strategy | Judge **concept, composition, visual hierarchy, clarity, and aesthetic quality** |

**Exceptions:** If the user explicitly asks for brand strategy, brand identity, or organization-level research, adapt the scope accordingly. Never assume these are required for a poster or invitation.

## Five core questions

1. **UNDERSTAND** — *What is the real communication problem?*  
   **搞清楚：这次设计到底要解决什么问题？**
2. **CREATIVE CONCEPT** — *What is the one meaningful idea?*  
   **想明白：这张作品最值得表达的一个想法是什么？**
3. **VISUAL PRINCIPLE** — *How can the idea become visible?*  
   **想办法：怎么用字体、形状和空间让想法被看见？**
4. **GRAPHIC SYSTEM** — *What rules make the idea repeatable?*  
   **定规则：这套视觉语言在当前作品里怎么保持一致？**
5. **DESIGN CRITIQUE** — *Does every decision serve the idea?*  
   **做判断：概念、构图、视觉重点和美感是否都成立？**

## Five stages, seven practical actions

**01 UNDERSTAND:** Research → Collect Observations  
**02 CREATIVE CONCEPT:** Find a Tension → Define the Idea  
**03 VISUAL PRINCIPLE:** Explore Visual Relationships → Sketch  
**04 GRAPHIC SYSTEM:** Define Rules → Generate/Execute → Test in requested formats  
**05 DESIGN CRITIQUE:** Critique → Refine → Iterate

The seven actions are **methods inside** the five stages, not a second separate consulting workflow. Move backward when critique reveals a deeper issue. Skip finished stages rather than starting over.

### 01 — UNDERSTAND: the assignment, not the organization

**Purpose:** Define exactly what this work must communicate.

- Read the user/client brief and available source material. Identify the **specific deliverable**, audience, message, required words, emotional tone, format, and restrictions.
- Do only enough contextual research to make good decisions for this assignment. If the brief already answers the question, **do not research it again**.
- Record useful observations about people, content, behavior, materials, and visual context. Separate client facts from your own inferences.
- Observe the current design context: is this a cocktail-hour invitation rather than a gala? A teen-focused fundraising event rather than a children's picture book? These are **assignment-specific** observations.
- Do not pursue organizational history, corporate strategy, “hidden truths,” or a new brand positioning unless the task actually requires it.

**Output:** One-sentence communication problem, short brief, 3–5 useful observations. Use [01-discovery.md](templates/01-discovery.md).

**Gate:** Do we know *what this piece should say, to whom, and how it should feel*?

### 02 — CREATIVE CONCEPT: one meaningful idea for this piece

**Purpose:** Find a fresh idea that belongs to this communication task.

- Turn the strongest observation into a creative opportunity. Seek a **tension** (contrast or relationship) only when it helps.
- Offer **3 distinct concept directions**, each grounded in the assignment: a one-sentence idea, reason it fits, intended audience response, and major risk.
- Concepts must be different **ideas**, not three styles or palettes.
- Never equate a medium (risograph), object (chair), symbol (speech bubble), or vague theme (hope, community) with a complete concept.
- If an idea is generic enough to work for any client, make it more specific or discard it.

**Output:** 3 concept cards, 1 recommended direction and why. Use [02-concept-cards.md](templates/02-concept-cards.md).

**Gate:** Does this concept give the work something particular and worth expressing?

### 03 — VISUAL PRINCIPLE: think in forms, not symbolic objects

**Purpose:** Discover a visual relationship that makes the concept visible.

- Start with **typography, shape, scale, contrast, alignment, rhythm, positive/negative space, repetition, overlap, balance, and transformation**.
- Ask “**What should change visually when the idea happens?**” rather than “What object symbolizes it?”
- Develop **3 genuinely different visual mechanisms** and make **6–9 black-and-white thumbnails** where appropriate.
- Keep subject, frame, and wording stable across comparable studies; change one visual variable at a time.
- Make sure the idea survives without color, texture, illustration, or a lengthy explanation.
- Physical objects and metaphors are optional; use one only when it is genuinely helpful and recognizable.
- Use [visual-operations.md](references/visual-operations.md) and [03-visual-explorations.md](templates/03-visual-explorations.md).

**Output:** Selected visual principle plus concrete composition sketches / layout descriptions.

**Gate:** Can someone see the concept **in the formal relationship itself**?

### 04 — GRAPHIC SYSTEM: just enough rules for this work

**Purpose:** Develop the chosen visual principle into coherent execution.

- Define **only the rules needed by the current deliverables**: type hierarchy, placement, spacing, recurring shape behavior, palette, texture, image treatment.
- Mark **fixed choices**, **flexible choices**, and **things to avoid**.
- Test required sizes/contexts only: e.g. a 5×7 invitation and a specified email banner, not an imaginary enterprise identity.
- Choose style/material after the idea: risograph, cut paper, photography and illustration are **execution decisions**, not creative concepts.
- Generate/render only when requested. Verify copy, dates, logos, proportions, print legibility and production specs; image generation alone does not guarantee print-ready output.
- Use [04-visual-system.md](templates/04-visual-system.md).

**Output:** A compact project visual-rule sheet and relevant finished studies.

**Gate:** Does the work feel intentionally designed and consistent **without adding decorative filler**?

### 05 — DESIGN CRITIQUE: look at the actual design

**Purpose:** Improve the specific image/artifact, not grade corporate strategy.

Assess:
1. **Concept** — Is the idea communicated, or only explained afterward?
2. **Composition** — Do balance, space, proportions, and layout work?
3. **Hierarchy** — What is seen first? Can all essential words be read?
4. **Visual quality** — Is the form, typography, color, texture and craft strong?
5. **Relevance** — Does this serve the client brief, audience and tone?
6. **Necessity** — Which elements earn their place? Remove irrelevant ones.

- Give actionable **Keep / Change / Remove** decisions.
- Identify the level of the problem: brief, concept, visual relationship, layout, system rule, or execution.
- When revising, **preserve every approved aspect that the user did not ask to change**.
- Do not defend a weak design with an elaborate symbolic narrative. Visual communication should be apparent.
- Use [05-critique.md](templates/05-critique.md) and [critique-checklist.md](references/critique-checklist.md).

**Output:** Concise critique, ranked improvements, one controlled next iteration.

**Gate:** Does every important choice strengthen communication or the formal quality of the work?

## Built-in anti-drift checks

Before writing any research, concept, prompt, or critique, silently check:

- **Task check:** Am I working on the requested artwork, or drifting into defining the whole company?
- **Evidence check:** Am I quoting a fact from the brief, or inventing a brand truth?
- **Concept check:** Is this an idea for the piece, or just an object, mood word, or design style?
- **Form check:** Did I actually explore typography, shape and space, or jump to symbolic illustrations?
- **System check:** Am I designing rules for deliverables that exist, or proposing an unrequested brand identity?
- **Critique check:** Am I judging the visible design rather than abstract organizational strategy?

**If drift occurs:** Return to the user's specific deliverable; restate the current problem in one sentence; resume from the latest useful approved decision. Do **not** launch a deeper company investigation.

## How to interact

- Match the requested stage; do not force users through every stage when they ask for a single design change.
- Work from provided files/briefs; don't invent client approval, brand requirements, testimonials, or facts.
- Be a practical creative partner: concise reasoning, different alternatives, visual specificity and honest judgment.
- When users request exploration, give genuine alternatives. When they request an edit, make the edit — not a brand redesign.
- Research is a **means**, not the deliverable; sketches and visible decisions matter.
- Prefer the smallest artifact useful now: concept cards, sketch directions, a print layout, or critique.
- For a worked application, see [example-lsc.md](references/example-lsc.md). It is illustrative, not client-approved work.
